# A Simple Checklist for Improving Your Small Business Website’s Accessibility

Small business owners often spend countless hours perfecting their websites to make them visually appealing and user-friendly. However, many fail to consider the accessibility of their sites – a critical aspect that ensures everyone can use your site, regardless of any disability or impairment they might have. In this article, we’ll focus on one specific area: where should you start when it comes to improving website accessibility? Here’s how you can make a difference in a straightforward and practical way.

## Understanding Website Accessibility

Website accessibility means making sure that your site is usable by as many people as possible, including those with disabilities. This includes users who are visually impaired, deaf or hard of hearing, have cognitive disabilities, or use assistive technologies like screen readers or voice recognition software to navigate web pages. By improving the accessibility of your website, you ensure that it meets legal requirements and opens up a larger audience.

## What to Fix First: Simple Changes for Better Accessibility

When starting with making your site more accessible, there are several quick fixes you can make right away:

### 1. **Ensure Keyboard Navigation**
   - **What to Do:** Make sure every interactive element on your website – like links and buttons – can be navigated using only a keyboard.
   - **Example:** If you have an image gallery with clickable thumbnails, users should be able to navigate through them by pressing the arrow keys.

### 2. **Optimize Text**
   - **What to Do:** Use proper HTML for headings (H1, H2, etc.) and alt text for images.
   - **Example:** For each main heading on your page, use `h1` tags. For any image that doesn’t display well without the text alternative, provide an `alt` attribute describing the content of the image.

### 3. **Add Skip Navigation Links**
   - **What to Do:** Create a shortcut link at the top or bottom of each web page that allows users to skip over repetitive navigation links and jump directly to what they need.
   - **Example:** A user with visual impairments can use this feature to avoid scrolling through unnecessary menu items.

### 4. **Provide Text Alternatives for Non-text Content**
   - **What to Do:** Use `alt` attributes for images, `longdesc` for complex graphics or large data tables, and transcripts for audio content.
   - **Example:** If you use an infographic on your page, include a detailed description in the `longdesc` attribute. For videos, provide captions.

### 5. **Ensure Proper Color Contrast**
   - **What to Do:** Use sufficient contrast between text and background colors so that individuals with visual impairments can read the content.
   - **Example:** Ensure there is at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio for most font sizes used on your site.

### 6. **Make Your Forms Accessible**
   - **What to Do:** Clearly label form fields and provide instructions, such as placeholder text or tooltips that describe each field’s purpose.
   - **Example:** For an input field asking for a password, include a prompt saying "Please enter your password."

### 7. **Implement ARIA Roles and Labels**
   - **What to Do:** Use `aria-label` attributes when there is no appropriate HTML element for an interactive element or when you want to add additional context.
   - **Example:** For a button with a specific function, label it like this: `<button aria-label="Make Reservation">Book Now</button>`.

## Conclusion
Improving the accessibility of your small business website doesn’t have to be complicated. By starting with these fundamental changes and following them up regularly, you can make your site more inclusive and beneficial for all visitors, including those who rely on assistive technologies or navigate differently. Remember, a little bit goes a long way in making your website accessible, ensuring that everyone has an equal opportunity to use it.

By implementing these simple yet impactful accessibility improvements, not only are you complying with legal requirements but also demonstrating a commitment to inclusivity and diversity.
